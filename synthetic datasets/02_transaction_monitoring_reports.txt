1. Overview
Transaction monitoring is a control function used by the bank to identify potentially unusual financial activity.
The Transaction Monitoring System analyzes transaction information using predefined business rules, customer risk profiles, historical activity, and other relevant contextual data.

Monitoring alerts are generated when observed activity satisfies specified detection conditions.An alert does not automatically establish suspicious behavior or criminal activity. Each alert must be assessed using the customer's circumstances, account history, supporting evidence, and applicable compliance requirements. This document provides synthetic monitoring records for three AML investigations.

7. Monitoring and Investigation Workflow
The operational workflow is:
Stage 1: Data ingestion
Transaction data is received from the bank's operational systems.

Stage 2: Customer context enrichment
The monitoring system associates transactions with customer, account, merchant, and beneficiary information.

Stage 3: Monitoring assessment
The system evaluates the activity against monitoring controls and customer-specific context.

Stage 4: Alert generation
Potentially unusual activity produces an alert.

Stage 5: Case management
The alert is reviewed and, where appropriate, converted into an AMLCase.

Stage 6: Investigation
The assigned investigator reviews transactions, KYC profiles, counterparties, and supporting evidence.

Stage 7: Compliance decision
Compliance personnel document the case disposition and any reporting obligations.

Stage 8: Ongoing monitoring
Relevant accounts or relationships may be subject to additional review according to institutional policy.
