7. Cross-Customer Relationship Management

Customer relationship management includes several interconnected operational processes.

Customer and Employee

Each customer may be assigned a relationship manager.

The relationship manager is responsible for customer servicing, relationship reviews, product coordination, and service escalation.

Customer and Branch

Each customer is associated with a primary servicing branch.

Branches support customer onboarding, account maintenance, document verification, and customer service.

Customer and Account

A customer may own one or multiple accounts.

Each account must reference its customer ownership relationship.

Customer and Financial Product

A customer may subscribe to multiple financial products.

Examples include savings accounts, loans, insurance policies, investment holdings, and digital wallets.

Customer and Compliance

Customer records are reviewed through KYC and compliance assessments.

Customer risk classifications may change following new information or periodic review.

Customer and AML

Customers may become subjects of AML investigations when transaction monitoring generates relevant alerts.

The existence of an AML case does not automatically establish a regulatory violation.

Customer and Consent

Customers may provide or withdraw consent for specified privacy processing activities, communications, and service-related data usage.

Consent must be maintained with its purpose, effective period, status, and source.

Customer and Complaint

Customer complaints create service records that may be connected to accounts, transactions, products, branches, and corrective actions.

8. Temporal Relationship Management

Customer relationships change over time.

OntoGraphRAG must preserve historical context when retrieving customer records.

Examples include:

A customer may have an active account today but previously maintained a closed account.
A customer's risk category may change following a compliance assessment.
An account may be associated with different servicing employees over time.
A KYC profile may be updated without changing the customer's identity.
A customer may enroll in additional financial products.
A complaint may be resolved while remaining part of the customer's historical record.

Each temporal relationship should ideally contain:

Relationship identifier.
Source entity.
Target entity.
Relationship type.
Valid-from date.
Valid-to date, where applicable.
Relationship status.
Source document reference.

Historical records must not be overwritten merely because a relationship changes.
